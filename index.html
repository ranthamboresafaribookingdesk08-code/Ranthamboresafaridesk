Ranthambore Safari Zones Guide (Zones 1 to 10)

Ranthambore Tiger Reserve is divided into 10 safari zones. Zones 1 to 5 are core forest areas known for lakes and fort ruins, while Zones 6 to 10 offer hilly terrains and scenic valleys with frequent tiger sightings.

Core Zone 1 to 5

Zones 1, 2 & 3 (Lake & Fort Zones)

Features iconic spots like Singhdwar, Jogi Mahal, Rajbagh Lake, and Padam Talao. Excellent for tiger sightings, sambar deer, and scenic fort backdrops.

Core Zone 1 to 5

Zones 4 & 5 (Lakes & Bakula Valley)

Home to lush green valleys, streams, and dense cover. Famous for active tiger movement, sloth bears, and thick banyan canopy areas.

Buffer Zone 6 to 10

Zones 6 & 7 (Kundu & Charanoda)

Hilly terrain adjacent to Zone 1. Offers great hill view landscapes, birdwatching, leopards, and territorial tiger movements.

Buffer Zone 6 to 10

Zones 8, 9 & 10 (Balas & Qual Ji)

Scenic rocky hills, river belts, and deep valleys. Known for tranquil forest routes, sloth bears, leopards, and tiger sightings.

Heritage & Legacy

The Historic Identity of Sawai Madhopur

Sawai Madhopur is not just the gateway to Royal Bengal Tigers; it is a land steeped in royal Rajput courage, ancient architecture, and rich Rajasthani traditions.

👑 Royal Origins & The Chauhan Dynasty

Founded in 1763 AD by Maharaja Sawai Madho Singh I of Jaipur, the city was planned as a fortified settlement near the legendary Ranthambore Fort. Before the 18th century, this region was dominated by the brave Chauhan Rajput rulers, most notably Rana Hammir Dev Chauhan, who stood as a symbol of sacrifice, honor, and valor against invading forces.

Today, Sawai Madhopur preserves this majestic past through UNESCO World Heritage sites, royal hunting pavilions, and sacred temples tucked away inside dense tiger territory.

Explore Sawai Madhopur

Top Tourist Places in & Around Sawai Madhopur

Beyond the adrenaline of tiger safaris, explore the ancient citadels, historic temples, peaceful lakes, and natural museums.

Ranthambore Fort

Located inside the national park, this 10th-century UNESCO World Heritage site offers majestic views of the entire tiger reserve forest.

Trinetra Ganesh Temple

Situated atop Ranthambore Fort, this famous 3-eyed Lord Ganesha temple is one of the oldest and most revered shrines in Rajasthan.

Surwal Lake & Bird Watching

A serene seasonal lake near Sawai Madhopur, perfect for morning sunrises and observing migratory birds like flamingos and cranes.

Jogi Mahal & Banyan Tree

An ancient royal hunting lodge situated on Padam Talao lake, home to India's second largest iconic Banyan Tree.
